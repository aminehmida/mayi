Twelfth alpha: each machine may use only the secrets you choose for it, and tags replace folders.

## ACLs

- **Each paired machine has an ACL: the secrets it may use**, as whole tags, single secrets, or all secrets. A request for anything else is refused at once, without asking you, and the refusal is listed in Activity. `mayi list` on that machine shows only what it may use.
- **Pairing asks for the ACL first**, with nothing ticked, and cannot continue until something is chosen. `mayi-devd serve` takes `pair TAG…`.
- **Agents on a Mac with the app have one too**, "Agents on <Mac>", for their requests through the app and from there to your other devices. It starts with all secrets.
- **Change an ACL under Machines on the Mac or Devices on the phone.** It reaches your other devices with the next sync, and each of them enforces it.
- **Machines paired before this release keep every secret** until you narrow their ACL.
- An ACL limits which machine may use a secret, not what an agent on that machine does with it: that is still the sandbox's job. Details in the [threat model](../threat-model.md#acls).

## Tags

- **A secret can carry several tags**, typed or picked when you add or edit it. Tags appear in the sidebar on the Mac and as filters on the phone, and search matches them.
- **A tag in an ACL covers secrets tagged later too.** So the editor shows "Usable by", the machines that could use the secret, as you type its tags.
- **Folders are gone.** A secret's folder is not turned into a tag; tag your secrets again where you want them grouped.

## Updating

Update every device: a device on alpha.11 or earlier does not know ACLs, so a request it answers is not checked against them. Quit Mayi on the Mac, `brew upgrade --cask mayi`, and allow the keychain prompt when Mayi starts. Update the phone through Obtainium, and `mayi` on other machines (`brew upgrade mayi-cli`).
